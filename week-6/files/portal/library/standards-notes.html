IFRS S2 (ISSB), with TCFD structure: note (v1)

Record ids. D-6-n is a Week 6 decision, J-n a journal entry, F-0n a review finding, DR6-n a data request, A-n an assumption; all are in the engagement record.

  • Version: IFRS S2, issued June 2023; effective for periods beginning on or after 1 January 2024 where adopted by a jurisdiction. Amendments to GHG emissions disclosures issued December 2025 (relief, including on financed emissions). Verified against the IFRS page, retrieved 2026-10-23: https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/ifrs-sustainability-standards-navigator/ifrs-s2-climate-related-disclosures/
  • Status as of 2026-10-23: voluntary for a US private fund manager; adopted or being adopted by many jurisdictions (UK SRS is based on it). TCFD disbanded in 2023 and handed monitoring to the ISSB.
  • Re-check by: 2027-04-30.
  • Structure: four pillars, eleven TCFD recommended disclosures; requirement ids S2-<pillar>-<letter> follow TCFD a/b/c inside each pillar. Paragraph numbers verified 2026-11-06 against AASB S2 (IFRS numbering); the IFRS text and the December 2025 amendments to 29(a) were not read. Do not copy the licensed text; cite by paragraph id.
  • Materiality: financial only (could the information reasonably be expected to influence investors). Not double materiality.

Common pitfalls

  • Scope 2 shown market-based only. S2 requires location-based, with contractual (market) information alongside.
  • Financed emissions (Scope 3 category 15) silently omitted by a fund. Say it is a gap, and why, rather than omit it.
  • Scenario analysis described as a forecast. It is a stress test of resilience.
  • "Financial effects" given only as qualitative words. Give ranges in dollars, with the period and method.

Defensible language

  • "We assessed resilience against two scenarios. This is not a forecast."
  • "Scope 3 is not yet measured. We expect to report category 15 from [year], subject to company data."
  • "Physical-risk losses are modelled estimates, shown as a range."

ESRS E1 (CSRD) climate change: note (v1, re-mapped 2026-10-23)

  • Version mapped: the simplified ESRS, Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1563 of 3 July 2026, amending 2023/2772 (OJ L 21 September 2026; http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2026/1563/oj). It applies to financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2027. It enters into force on 10 November 2026 (Article 3, read on EUR-Lex 2026-11-04). For financial years starting in 2026, undertakings in scope may apply the 2023 standards (as amended by Regulation (EU) 2025/1416), the new ones, or the 2023 standards with a list of reliefs, and must say which they apply (Article 2). E1 now has 11 disclosure requirements (E1-1 to E1-11). Ids and titles were verified on EUR-Lex on 2026-10-23; the mapping to register concepts is the scout's reading.
  • What moved (2023 id, then the new id): transition plan E1-1 stays E1-1; risk identification and scenario analysis (was ESRS 2 IRO-1) is now E1-2; resilience (was ESRS 2 SBM-3) is now E1-3; policies E1-2 to E1-4; actions E1-3 to E1-5; targets E1-4 to E1-6; energy E1-5 to E1-7; Scope 1 to 3 E1-6 to E1-8; removals and credits E1-7 to E1-9; internal carbon pricing E1-8 to E1-10; anticipated financial effects E1-9 to E1-11. The 2023 mapping is kept in _archive/esrs-e1-requirements-set1-2023-v1.csv, for any company that applies the 2023 set for financial year 2026 (Article 2 lets companies in scope choose) or is still reporting 2025 on it.
  • Dropped, as far as a text search shows: GHG intensity per net revenue and energy intensity for high climate impact sectors. No intensity datapoint was found in E1-7 or E1-8. Confirm before telling an EU reader it is not required.
  • Scope (Directive (EU) 2026/470, Omnibus I, OJ 26 February 2026, in force; thresholds verified against the operative articles on 2026-10-23):
    • An undertaking, group or issuer is in scope when its net turnover exceeds EUR 450m and it has more than 1,000 employees on average. The new scope applies to financial years starting on or after 1 January 2027.
    • Non-EU groups: an EU subsidiary or branch reports for the group when the group's EU turnover exceeds EUR 450m in each of the last two financial years (up from EUR 150m) and the EU subsidiary has turnover over EUR 200m (a branch counts only if there is no such subsidiary). Derogation that matters for private equity: where the non-EU parent is a financial holding undertaking whose subsidiaries' businesses are independent of one another, member states must let those subsidiaries and branches decide not to publish. A fund holding unrelated portfolio companies may fit this; get a legal reading.
    • Value-chain cap: a reporting company cannot require a supplier or customer with 1,000 employees or fewer to provide more than the voluntary standard covers (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1560, OJ 21 September 2026, in force).
  • Status as of 2026-11-04: EC CSRD page, EUR-Lex (Articles 2 and 3 of 2026/1563 read on 2026-11-04). Re-check by: 2027-01-31.
  • Who it reaches here: a US mid-market GP and most of its companies are likely outside direct scope on the facts known; the screen marks this unknown until the entity facts and counsel's reading are in. The likely routes are:
    • a portfolio company with a large EU sub-group (rule A-04);
    • a US group with large EU turnover (A-04c);
    • EU customers' requests, capped at the voluntary standard for companies of 1,000 employees or fewer (A-05).

Double materiality vs IFRS S2. S2 asks what climate does to the company (financial). E1 also asks what the company does to the climate (impact). A topic can be material for E1 on impact alone. See ../methodology/materiality-scoring-v1.md.

Common pitfalls: treating an S2 report as E1-compliant; citing the 2023 E1 numbering after 2027; netting carbon credits against gross emissions (E1-9 keeps them separate); asking a small supplier for more than the value-chain cap allows.

Defensible language: "This report follows IFRS S2. Where an EU investor or customer needs ESRS E1 data points, we map them in the content index. We do not claim ESRS compliance."

SFDR principal adverse impact (PAI) indicators: note (v1)

  • Version: SFDR Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/1288, Annex I Table 1 (mandatory indicators; climate and environment are 1 to 9) and Table 2 #4 (optional). https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R1288. Numbering and names verified on EUR-Lex 2026-10-23 (browser). Indicator 6 is in GWh per EUR million of revenue; the register holds MWh and USD, so state the conversion and exchange-rate date.
  • The 20 November 2025 proposal (EC Q&A, https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/qanda_25_2737, read 2026-10-23) deletes the entity-level PAI statement and its templates. Today, financial market participants with more than 500 employees must publish one. Product-level disclosures would be cut back to indicators chosen by the fund. On 2026-10-23 the EC SFDR page still listed it as a proposal. If adopted, most EU LP requests for these indicators would fall away: re-tier this file then.
  • Status as of 2026-10-23: applies to EU financial market participants. A US GP is not in scope, but EU LPs need these indicators from it to complete their own PAI statements.
  • Re-check by: 2027-01-31.
  • Pitfalls: PAI uses EUR and enterprise value including cash (EVIC) for attribution; a US fund reports USD and may lack EVIC for private companies. State the currency, the exchange-rate date and the proxy used for EVIC.
  • Defensible language: "Indicators are provided to support LPs' own PAI statements. Attribution uses [method]; values for private companies use [proxy]."

ILPA / ESG Data Convergence Initiative (EDCI): note (v1)

  • Version: EDCI core metrics as named on ILPA's EDCI page (https://ilpa.org/industry-guidance/environmental-social-governance/data-convergence-initiative/, retrieved 2026-10-23): greenhouse gas emissions, net zero commitment, renewable energy, board and C-suite diversity, work-related accidents, net new hires and employee engagement. ILPA has been the secretariat since 2022. Verified: the metric names. Unverified (from model knowledge): the detailed definitions (scope split, optional Scope 3, data-type flags). The page states no current metric year. The old EDCI hostnames did not resolve.
  • Status: voluntary, LP-driven; the most common GP-to-LP template in US private equity. Annual collection cycle.
  • Re-check by: 2027-04-30, and get the current metric definitions document from ILPA before first client use.
  • Pitfalls: mixing location- and market-based Scope 2 across companies; reporting estimated emissions without flagging them as estimated (EDCI asks for the data type).
  • Defensible language: "Figures follow EDCI definitions for [year]; estimated values are flagged and graded C."

Tier 2, Tier 3 and methodology standards: note (v1)

Machine-readable list: tier2-3-frameworks-v1.csv (one row per framework: status, who it reaches, route, pillar-level map to register concepts, source, retrieval date, re-check date).

  • Tier 2 is applicability plus a pillar-level map: enough to tell a client whether it is in scope and which register concepts it draws on, not a paragraph-level content index. Upgrade a framework to Tier 1 when a client is actually in scope.
  • Tier 3 (monitor only): US SEC climate rule. Status checked 2026-10-23 against SEC press release 2025-58: the SEC stopped defending it. Do not build for it; re-check at each engagement start.
  • Methodology standards (GHG Protocol, PCAF) are cited in the methodology note of each report, not mapped.
  • Rows marked unverified: from model knowledge must be checked against the official page before client use. The CARB row was checked on 2026-10-23; the proposed SB 253 deadline is itself still a proposal.
