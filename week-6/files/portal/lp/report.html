Northwood Capital climate report for limited partners

Report date: 2026-11-08. Reporting period: 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31. Prepared from Northwood's climate data table; every figure in the text is drawn from that table.

Basis of preparation

Emissions data reflects management estimates based on portfolio company-reported energy consumption and EPA emission factors. Data has not been independently verified by a third-party assurance provider.

Scope 3 is estimated from spend; financed emissions use PCAF attribution with stated proxies.

Year status: Year 1 baseline: no prior published report, so there are no prior-year comparatives.

Portfolio companies are not named; where one is described, it is only as a Fund I or Fund II portfolio company.

Bracketed codes such as [S2-GOV-a] point to the requirement in the framework content indices that accompany this report.

Governance

Who oversees climate risk

Boundary: Northwood and the ten portfolio companies held at the end of the reporting period.

This report names Sarah Mitchell, Chief Operating Officer of Northwood, as the executive responsible for climate-related disclosure and for acting on what it shows [S2-GOV-a]. Northwood has not yet formally confirmed the governance arrangements; that confirmation is on its list of actions. Northwood's climate data protocol provides for a VP of ESG & Sustainability to prepare the content and for Investor Relations to review it before it reaches LPs; no one holds the VP title in Northwood's team records [S2-GOV-b]. Board-level oversight of climate-related risks is absent at 10 of 10 companies, and a dedicated sustainability role is absent at 10 of 10 companies [S2-GOV-a] [S2-GOV-b]. Environmental matters sit with facilities, finance, quality or compliance leads as part of other jobs. We state this plainly because buyers and LPs ask who owns climate risk, and a named owner with a clear mandate is part of being ready for a sale. Whether to create committee-level oversight is a decision for Northwood; this report does not assume one.

Strategy

Financial effects of physical climate risk

Boundary: All ten portfolio companies, from the reporting date to each company's expected exit; physical hazards; gross of insurance.

Modelled physical climate risk could cost the portfolio about $12.6M in expected losses over the companies' holding periods in the central case, within a range of $4.0M to $26.8M [S2-STR-b3]. Because limited partners commit by fund, the figure is also split by fund: $1.9M for the Fund I companies (range $0.7M to $4.3M) and $10.7M for the Fund II companies (range $3.3M to $22.6M) [S2-STR-b3]. The fund figures are rounded, so their ranges may not add exactly to the portfolio range. The figure covers physical hazards only, is gross of insurance recoveries and recovered revenue, and is dated as shown in the metrics table; who would pay is estimated by rule and is not part of it [S2-STR-b3]. Hazards were screened by site, region and time horizon for every company [S2-STR-a]. Scenarios included hurricane landfalls, drought, winter storm, extreme heat and a supplier outage. The model was calibrated against disruption events that companies reported between 2023 and 2026; assumptions were adjusted where it did not at first reproduce them, and some reported events could not be tested, so calibration is a check on scale, not a guarantee. Scenario analysis covers physical hazards only; no transition scenario is reported here. These are expected values, not forecasts, and the range between the low and high cases is wide. A single storm may affect every site in a region at once. For some companies, source data limits the estimate.

Where climate touches revenue and supply

Boundary: All ten portfolio companies; transition and value-chain exposure as modelled for EBITDA; upper what-if cases, not expected losses.

For 4 of 10 companies the largest modelled exposure is a customer's requirement for carbon data, which ties a climate metric directly to revenue; for 6 of 10 companies it is concentration among a few suppliers [S2-STR-b1]. These are upper what-if cases built for ranking exposures, not expected losses, and they are not added to the physical-risk figure above.

Risk management

How climate risk is found and managed

Boundary: Northwood's annual climate data process across the ten portfolio companies.

Northwood sends each company a collection template in January. Companies return energy and fuel data and a climate questionnaire by mid-March, and the portfolio footprint is due by mid-April, as the protocol sets out. Northwood's team validates the data, estimates gaps with stated proxies and grades quality A, B or C [S2-RM-a]. Risks are expressed in dollars, not scores [S2-RM-a]. Options to reduce risk are ranked by financial return and by timing against each company's exit [S2-RM-b]. The process is documented in Northwood's protocol and this report is its first full output; how each company fits climate risk into its own risk review has not yet been collected and is on the list of requests to close [S2-RM-c].

Metrics and targets

Emissions and energy

Boundary: Scope 1 and 2 for all ten portfolio companies, operational control, calendar 2025, each company counted for the months Northwood owned it; Scope 3 estimated from spend for the twelve months to January 2026 on the same ownership basis; financed emissions attributed under PCAF.

Portfolio Scope 1 emissions were 11,599 tCO2e and Scope 2 location-based emissions were 10,179 tCO2e, each counting two companies only for the months Northwood owned them [S2-MT-a1]. The industry data convention (ILPA EDCI) asks for whole-company figures with no ownership adjustment; those full-year company figures are held internally and the totals here are ownership-period totals [EDCI-1] [EDCI-2]. Energy consumption was 83,091 MWh on the same ownership basis; the renewable share is not reported [EDCI-4]. Emissions intensity was 13.7 tCO2e/USDm: the full-year Scope 1 and 2 emissions of all ten companies (23,437 tCO2e, more than the ownership-period total above because the two part-year companies count for the whole year) divided by their combined revenue for the twelve months to January 2026 ($1,715.0M) [S2-MT-a1]. Scope 3 emissions are estimated at about 289,434 tCO2e on the same ownership basis, graded C [S2-MT-a1] [EDCI-3]. The estimate covers operating purchases (mainly purchased goods and services, with transport and business travel); capital goods are not captured, since capex is outside operating cost. For the four clinic, office and staffing companies the purchases are mainly rent (closer to category 8, and partly already in Scope 1 and 2). The other Scope 3 categories are not estimated. One month of each company's costs, scaled to its revenue for the twelve months to January 2026, is reduced by an estimate of pay (sector average pay for its headcount, or the company's own cost of revenue where that is mostly pay) and multiplied by EPA supply-chain emission factors. The range of 267,614 tCO2e to 303,981 tCO2e reflects the pay assumption only, not factor or spend uncertainty; it is not a confidence interval. Some of the spend is energy and rent, part of which is already counted in Scope 1 and 2, so the two totals should not be added. Factors priced in earlier-year dollars push the estimate up, while using one supplier sector per company can move it either way, and one company accounts for about 43% of the total. The figure is much larger than Scope 1 and 2 because it counts emissions in the companies' supply chains rather than in their own operations. Financed emissions following the PCAF listed and unlisted equity method, with proxies (equity at entry for total equity; current debt) were 12,494 tCO2e: 5,380 tCO2e for the Fund I companies and 7,114 tCO2e for the Fund II companies [S2-MT-a2] [PAI-1]. They are each company's full-year Scope 1 and 2 emissions multiplied by the fund's share of its capital, measured as the fund's equity invested at entry over total equity at entry plus debt today; that share averages about half, which is why the figure is about half of the companies' full-year Scope 1 and 2. Market-based Scope 2 has not been calculated; it needs the companies' electricity contracts, which are on Northwood's list of data requests [S2-MT-a1]. The GHG Protocol asks for both location- and market-based Scope 2; only location-based is reported. Market-based needs confirmation of any supply contracts or certificates (otherwise residual-mix factors apply). The figures are management estimates from company-reported energy use and published emission factors, not audited.

Targets

Boundary: All ten portfolio companies and the portfolio as a whole.

No portfolio company has set an emissions reduction goal with a baseline year: 10 of 10 companies answered no, and a company that is evaluating science-based goals has not yet set one [S2-MT-t]. No portfolio-wide goal exists, and Northwood has not yet set a date for one. This report's year is the first baseline and may allow goals to be set against it.
