Executive Summary

In one paragraph. Northwood's climate protocol exists on paper but has not run for 2026. All ten portfolio companies fall in the lowest readiness band (26 to 38 out of 100). Meridian, in a live $363.9M to $400.9M exit process, is the most urgent. Four fixes protect the most value: reconciling Meridian's exit data, Harvest Ridge's resubmission (about $34.0M of revenue), Sterling's facility-level traceability ($25.0M to $42.0M of defense RFPs) and Northwood's own LP climate section.

Reading the references. D-numbers (D1 to D45) are entries in the disposition log, one per finding with its evidence. A-numbers (A1 to A29) are entries in the assumption log, one per judgment call with its rationale. NW-numbers are items for Northwood to answer; company codes such as NC-6 are asks in that company's data request. Labels in brackets (Confirmed, Reported, Inferred) say how firmly each statement is sourced.

Northwood's climate protocol exists on paper, but there is no sign in the data room that its 2026 cycle has run. No portfolio company has the Tier 2 climate analysis or board climate materials the protocol specifies. The ESG score tables under the new ESG Risk Assessment Methodology hold header rows only. The Q4 2025 LP letter carries no climate section, although the protocol requires one. Climate appears in board or value-creation materials at only 2 of 10 portcos. (Confirmed for the data room Northwood shared; work held outside it wouldn't show here.) The next hard deadline is the Q4 2026 LP letter. The most valuable one is Meridian's confirmatory diligence, due in Q4 2026 on an enterprise value of $363.9M to $400.9M (as at 31 January 2026).

The question "is our data ready?" has two different answers, depending on who is asking:

  • For LP disclosure: achievable for FY 2025, once known errors are fixed. Northwood's protocol allows LP climate figures to be management estimates, provided they carry its standard qualification language. The underlying energy data exists for all 10 portcos, and estimates are acceptable. Errors are not. Correctable errors at 6 portcos currently distort the portfolio total: NovaCare's template gas total is 90.5% above its questionnaire's, driven by two misfiled clinic rows, and Harvest Ridge's two submissions disagree on every total. FY 2025 will be the first baseline, so there is no year-over-year trend to report yet, and no portco has a target to report.
  • For buyer diligence: no portco would pass today. All 10 score in the lowest readiness band, 26 to 38 out of 100. Meridian is tied with Sterling for closest, has the most traceable data in the portfolio, and is the most urgent. Two CFO-signed submissions disagree on its gas and fleet figures, and the exit plan's vendor diligence scope has no ESG workstream.

Ranked by value at stake rather than by data urgency, four issues call for action now:

  1. Meridian's exit data. Reconcile its two submissions against utility invoices before the LOI, on a live $363.9M to $400.9M process (as at 31 January 2026).
  2. Harvest Ridge's resubmission. One clean submission protects about $34.0M of revenue tied to 2027 customer renewals (as at February 2026), and unblocks its baseline.
  3. Sterling's facility-level traceability. It underpins $25.0M to $42.0M of defense RFPs, with Boeing Defense's requirement mandatory from 2027.
  4. Northwood's own LP climate section. The protocol requires one in the Q4 2026 letter, due in mid-February 2027.

Three more carry high value but need a planned, resourced response rather than a data fix: Harvest Ridge's supplier programme, Pinnacle's refinery clients (about $18.0M of revenue (as at 12 December 2025)) and Ridgeline's sustainability services line ($1.5M to $8.0M a year of revenue, sized by its board). Several further items are cheap to fix. They include an FY 2026 insurance budget gap at Pinnacle and Northwood's collection template, which affects all 10 portcos from January 2027. The next section sets out the full ranking and who owns each item.

Figures followed by an "as at" date are older than 90 days, which Northwood's own data-sources standard requires us to flag. Almost every figure in this memo is that old; the vintage table in Scope and Method shows the source and date behind the headline figures.

Where Northwood's Attention Should Go

This memo ranks the findings in two ways, because they answer different questions:

  • By data urgency (Tiers 1 to 3, in the data requests): what must arrive first for the Week 3 baseline to be built. This sets the order of the data chase.
  • By value (below): where Northwood's attention and management time should go. Each issue is assessed on financial materiality, the value lever it touches, the external clock it runs against, the effort to fix it, the confidence in the exposure and who owns it.

The two rankings diverge, and that is the point:

  • Sterling's traceability doesn't block the baseline (Tier 2), but it carries one of the two largest revenue exposures in the portfolio.
  • The NovaCare, Patriot and Keystone data errors must be fixed first (Tier 1), but carry no direct financial exposure. They matter because they flow into Northwood's LP figures.

How the ranking works.

  • Materiality thresholds are scaled to Northwood portcos. High means $1.0M or more of EBITDA a year, $10.0M or more of revenue, or a live transaction. Medium means $0.1M to $1.0M of EBITDA, or $1.0M to $10.0M of revenue. These thresholds were set on September 25, 2026, after the findings were known. Next cycle they will be registered before any data is reviewed.
  • Priority rules:
  • Act now: high value, a clock within about a year, and a fix within reach.
  • Resource and plan: high value, but a fix that needs months or new capability.
  • Quick win: moderate value at low effort, or a cheap fix to Northwood's reported figures.
  • Enablers: an issue that has to be fixed before a higher-priority one inherits at least Quick win.
  • Dollars at stake are shown as each company or Northwood reports them (revenue, EBITDA or cost), not translated into enterprise value. Full-loss valuations would overstate exposure. Kith holds indicative enterprise-value translations in its working files, for discussion only. Meridian's exposure is left unquantified on purpose: the risk is diligence friction in a live process, not a calculable discount.

Scope and Method

One process, applied evenly across the portfolio

All 10 active portcos went through the same process at the same depth:

  • Automated checks on all 138 facility rows: identifiers, duplicates, blanks, source labels, outliers and row structure.
  • Reconciliation of every carbon template figure against the same company's questionnaire, at the finest level each questionnaire allows.
  • A keyword sweep of every portco's board decks, budgets, value-creation plans and KPI reporting for climate-relevant content, with each relevant passage read in full.
  • Every finding logged with evidence (D1 to D45), and every judgment call logged with its rationale (A1 to A29). D41 to D45 and A26 to A29 are a Stage 0 closeout run on September 27, 2026, after v4: files never opened in the original build, checked against what the memo already claimed.
  • An independent second extraction of all 10 questionnaires, to check the transcription behind every comparison. It found 151 shared figures and 0 mismatches.
  • A re-run of every script from the raw data, with the memo's figures checked against the fresh outputs before issue.

Data vintage. The newest operating data in Northwood's files is from January 2026 (monthly packages for all 10 portcos), and board materials are from Q4 2025. Events dated after that, such as Q3 2026 renewals and updates, are shown as due, with current status unconfirmed.

How the score works

Each portco is scored 0 to 4 on eight weighted dimensions, then combined into a composite out of 100.

Four points of interpretation:

  • Confidence levels. "Verified, source quality unknown" means the checks against the submitted files are certain, but no primary invoice has been seen. "Self-reported" rests on the portco's own statements. "Estimated" rests on risk scores whose provider is unknown. No score in this memo is verified with certainty. Findings below carry the same distinction: Confirmed (established directly from the files), Reported (the portco's statement, unverified) or Inferred (Kith's interpretation).
  • The ceiling today is about 63. Dimensions 3 to 6 (calculation, targets, governance, frameworks) carry 40.0% of the weight. No portco scores above 1 on any of them, and 6 of 10 score zero on all four. So even with perfect energy data, no portco could currently exceed about 63. A reading of "all 10 in the lowest band" partly reflects that. The real differences between portcos lie in dimensions 1, 2, 7 and 8.
  • Bands sit close together. Sterling and Meridian are 2.5 points from Partially ready, on unrounded composites of 37.50 (the table shows whole numbers). Both moved below the line when a governance rule was tightened during this audit: naming a person who compiles data no longer counts as governance (assumption log A7). Treat their band as a close call. The dimension profile is the more reliable read.
  • The weights were set with the data in view. They were fixed on September 22, 2026, after the first automated checks had run. They are held unchanged until data request responses arrive, so they can't be tuned to results.

How this relates to Northwood's ESG Risk Assessment Methodology

Northwood's methodology scores residual ESG risk from 1 (minimal) to 5 (high). This memo scores data readiness from 0 (absent) to 4 (complete and verifiable). They are different measures and run in opposite directions: a higher readiness score is better, and a higher risk score is worse. The readiness score tells Northwood how far to trust a risk score built on the same data.

Data Requests Accompanying This Memo

There is one request per portfolio company, all issued through Northwood. Each contains only that company's own data, with no scores or comparisons, per the protocol's Tier 1 access rule.

Every ask is placed in one of three tiers by a single rule, so portco teams know what to do first:

TierTestDue
1. Blocks the Week 3 baselineWithout it, a baseline figure would rest on a known conflict or gap of 5.0% or more of the company's electricity, gas or fleet fuel, or on a figure implausible on its face at 5.0% or more of the footprint; or it blocks issuing a request5 business days
2. Baseline proceeds provisionallyResolving it changes figures by less than 5.0%, or changes source grade, traceability or scope classification10 business days
3. Needed after Week 3No effect on Week 3 figures; informs materiality, risk and disclosure work in Weeks 4 to 610 business days; partial answers accepted

The tiers set sequence, not importance. Sterling has no Tier 1 asks, because its facility split doesn't change its total, yet its Tier 2 invoices matter most for its defense customers. Harvest Ridge's supplier evidence is Tier 3, though about $34.0M of revenue depends on it.

This timetable depends on the memo being reviewed and the requests issued on Monday, September 28, 2026. Tier 1 responses are then due Monday, October 5, after quarter-end close; Tiers 2 and 3 by Monday, October 12.

The ten requests, their contacts and their ask counts by tier are listed in the accompanying fund-level request (data-request-northwood-fund-level-2026-09-v3-draft.md). The full register of asks, each with its tier, the finding it traces to, the evidence that would satisfy it and the reason for its tier, is finding-criticality-register-v3.csv (79 asks).

Appendix B: How to Re-Run Next Quarter

Every step is scripted or logged. The scripts use only the Python standard library. The year, file paths, contacts and dates are set in one file, pipeline-config-v1.json. Judgment inputs live in data files, not code.

  1. Place new submissions in the protocol locations: the carbon template under _data/carbon/ and each portco's questionnaire under climate/_submitted/. Set the new year in pipeline-config-v1.json.
  2. Record an inventory of the data room and its data vintage, and register thresholds before reviewing any data (methodology v2, Stages 0 and 0b).
  3. Run verify-climate-data-v2.py for automated checks across all facilities.
  4. Transcribe each questionnaire's figures into the questionnaire extract, with a citation per row. Run a second, independent extraction and compare the two with crosscheck-questionnaire-extracts-v1.py.
  5. Run reconcile-carbon-vs-questionnaire-v3.py. It reconciles the template against the questionnaires for every portco and recovers malformed rows by a stated rule.
  6. Sweep each portco's board, budget and value-creation documents for climate-relevant content, and read the relevant passages in full.
  7. Log every flagged item in the disposition log, with evidence. Log every judgment call in the assumption log.
  8. Score each dimension per climate-data-readiness-rubric-v1.md in the scores file, with a citation for every score. Run score-composite-v2.py and grade-distribution-v3.py.
  9. Update finding-criticality-register-v3.csv and prioritisation-inputs-v1.csv. Run prioritise-findings-v2.py, then build-data-requests-v2.py to generate the requests (company text is in request-narratives-v1.md).
  10. Update this memo, then run verify-pipeline-v2.py. It re-runs every script and checks the memo's figures against the outputs; any failure blocks issue.

Emission factors, with source and retrieval date, are in _reference/emission-factors/. The process is set out in _reference/audit-methodology-v2.md.

Known limits: questionnaire extraction is still a manual transcription, checked by a second extraction. The playbook still lives in Kith's working folder; moving it to a Northwood-owned location is part of the Week 6 handover.
