02Why this matters

A carbon charge would be small. What a buyer asks for is the risk.

A climate matter is financially material to a Northwood company when it plausibly moves EBITDA by 5 percent or more, or is likely to be raised by a buyer, lender or large customer in diligence. On that test the carbon charge is small; the question a buyer asks about the number is not.

Carbon price
$761K a year

The whole portfolio's emissions priced at the California auction price (32.48 USD a tonne) is 0.29 percent of portfolio EBITDA. At the EU benchmark (82.98 USD) it is $1.94M, 0.75 percent. No Northwood company pays the EU price; it shows how a European or multinational buyer prices carbon.

EBITDA: every company stays under 5 percent. Emissions would have to be about 2.45 times larger at the EU price before any company leaves Northwood's Low band.

Data a buyer can trust
78.0% on bills or fuel

Grade C (estimates, landlord or proxy data) covers 11.7 percent of the portfolio. A Grade C company hands a buyer an unverified number, which invites questions and delay.

Exit readiness: collecting twelve months of utility bills at the Grade C companies is the cheapest step toward a clean diligence story.

Equipment risk at Sterling
R-22 rooftop unit

Sterling's 1995 R-22 unit at Akron is a phased-out refrigerant system the company has deferred replacing in two capital cycles and has had serviced in an emergency three times in 2025 (company questionnaire). The exposure is the failed or deferred replacement, not the tonnage.

Deal risk: a buyer will price the replacement, and heat treatment and finishing depend on the unit.

03The number

23,400 t CO2e, and the range behind it.

Scope 1 (fuel burned at sites and in vehicles) is 12,300 t and Scope 2 (the emissions behind purchased electricity, using grid-average factors for each site's region) is 11,100 t. The range runs from 22,000 t (sites the companies' own records cannot confirm are removed) to 23,600 t (sites the records contradict are added back). On the ownership-period basis the fund roll-up uses, the total is 21,800 t.

09ESG risk scorecard

Eight dimensions, ten companies, and where the evidence stops.

Northwood's methodology scores eight dimensions of risk, grouped as Environmental (E), Social (S) and Governance (G), from 1 (minimal risk) to 5 (high). A score is given only where the data room supports it; a blank cell says what evidence would allow one. 59 of 80 cells are scored and 21 are blank. This is Kith's judgment from submitted documents, except E2, which is built on the signed carbon figures. Prepared for Sarah Mitchell, not for the portfolio companies themselves.

13Why you can trust these numbers

157 findings were raised on this build. Every one has a recorded disposition.

Each reviewer owns one question and works read-only from the same packet. Findings are recorded as they were raised and every change of status cites the entry that resolved it. The run log behind the figures has 279 entries in a hash chain, so an edited or deleted entry is detectable.

Caught on this scorecard

  • An unsourced claim that a company "reports no violations": removed, with the cell's reliance on absence stated.
  • A tier A data file (a primary data-room source) the analyst had not opened, which contradicted three dashboard figures for Harvest Ridge: now opened, cited and labelled Conflicted.
  • A turnover benchmark of 20 percent that no source gave: removed.
  • A community-programs line missed for Patriot: the cell is now scored.
