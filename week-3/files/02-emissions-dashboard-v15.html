1. Why: what this number means for value as at 31 December 2025, over 90 days old as at 31 January 2026, over 90 days old

Pricing the portfolio's emissions at a carbon price would cost 0.29% to 0.75% of portfolio EBITDA, so carbon price is not what makes this inventory worth having. It is worth having because a buyer, lender or large customer will ask what the numbers are, where they come from and what is missing, and the answer decides how smoothly a company gets through diligence at exit. This page sizes material financial risk in that sense: what carbon and energy prices would cost, how much of the data a buyer could verify, and where the gaps are.

Even at the EU benchmark price, emissions would have to be about 2.45 times the base figure before any company leaves Northwood's Low band. If the cost reached EBITDA at exit, at Northwood's own exit multiples (8.5 to 10.5 times) the mid case is worth about $6.88M of enterprise value and the EU benchmark case about $17.60M. That is a what-if, not a forecast.

Working definition for this engagement: a climate matter is financially material to a Northwood company when it plausibly moves EBITDA by 5 percent or more, or is likely to be raised by a buyer, lender or large customer in diligence.

Carbon price

$761K a year

The whole portfolio's emissions priced at the California auction price ($32.48 a tonne): 0.29% of portfolio EBITDA. At the EU benchmark price it is $1.94M, 0.75%; no Northwood company pays that price, it shows how a European or multinational buyer prices carbon. All fall in Northwood's Low severity band (under 5 percent of EBITDA).

EBITDA: under these scenarios carbon price stays below 5 percent of EBITDA for every company. The largest single case is Harvest Ridge Foods at 2.04% of its EBITDA, at the EU benchmark price.

Energy cost

$879K a year

A 20 percent rise in energy prices (a stress case, not a forecast) costs this much on the energy spend the companies reported (0.34% of portfolio EBITDA). Reported spend covers only part of the portfolio and some of it only at headquarters, so this understates the total.

EBITDA: energy spend is reported by five of the ten companies, so this covers part of the portfolio only. Harvest Ridge Foods (1.67% of EBITDA) and Sterling Precision Manufacturing (1.19%) carry the most. Twenty percent is a round stress level with no external source.

Data a buyer can trust

78.0% of emissions on utility bills or fuel records

Grade C (estimates, landlord or proxy data) covers 11.7% of the portfolio. Keystone Behavioral Health, NovaCare Health Partners and Patriot Staffing Solutions are Grade C at company level.

Exit readiness: a Grade C company hands a buyer an unverified number. Collecting utility bills at these companies is a low-cost first step toward a cleaner diligence story.

Equipment risk at Sterling

R-22 rooftop unit

Sterling's R-22 rooftop A/C unit (SPM-001, Akron, 1995 vintage, about 50 lb) is flagged as a phase-out and replacement item. Equipment risk, not tonnage, is what a buyer cares about: the unit has been deferred in two capital cycles, needed emergency service three times in 2025, and costs an estimated $15-25K a year more to run than a modern system. Replacement is estimated at $180K.

Deal risk: the exposure is the failed or deferred replacement, not the tonnage (3.99 t CO2e a year of R-22, an estimate from EPA's default leak rate, reported beside the total, not in it).

What to do with this

  1. Collect utility bills at Keystone Behavioral Health, NovaCare Health Partners and Patriot Staffing Solutions, so their figures move from estimates to data a buyer can verify (exit readiness). Proposed owner: Northwood with each company; proposed effort: one request per company for twelve months of utility bills; proposed date: before issue.
  2. Decide when Sterling's R-22 rooftop unit is replaced: it is a $180K item the company has deferred twice, and a buyer will price it (deal risk). On the running-cost saving alone the simple payback is about 7 to 12 years, so the case is the failure risk a buyer will weigh, not the payback. Proposed owner: Northwood with Sterling; proposed date: before any sale process starts.
  3. Close the roster gaps before issue: the sites company records name that the carbon template leaves out (27: NovaCare 15, Patriot 10, Trident 1 and Ridgeline 1, the Las Vegas office) and the NovaCare clinic count (30 or 28). Each is a known understatement a buyer would find (diligence). Proposed owner: Northwood with each company; proposed date: before issue.
  4. Ask Clearview, Pinnacle, NovaCare, Keystone and Trident for their annual energy spend, so the energy figure covers all ten companies (EBITDA). Proposed owner: Northwood; proposed date: next annual collection.

This page supplies three of the four inputs to Northwood's transition-risk (E2) score: carbon intensity of operations, exposure to carbon pricing and energy-cost sensitivity. The other two, carbon intensity of customers and customers in fossil-fuel-dependent sectors, are not built here; Pinnacle's oil and gas customer concentration (its KPI dashboard) is the first place to look.

3. Can a buyer trust it? Data quality as at 31 December 2025, over 90 days old

Why it matters: a buyer's diligence team starts with the data behind a number. A Grade A company can hand over bills; a Grade C company hands over an estimate, which invites questions and delay at exit.

Grades follow Northwood's protocol, by share of emissions: A when more than 80 percent comes from utility bills or fuel records, B when more than 80 percent comes from bills plus accounting data, otherwise C. A company with exactly 20 percent estimates is graded C. Grade C data is flagged wherever it appears.

8. Method, assurance and open items as at 31 December 2025, over 90 days old

Why the number holds up

  • Rules fixed before the numbers were seen. The roster, source order, ownership period, denominators, fleet test, refrigerant treatment, grading and the structure of the exposure scenarios were registered and locked before any calculation. The carbon prices were selected as of the report date (the latest auction before it, the latest full year before it) and the energy shock was fixed at the same time, on 4 October 2026, after the emissions calculation was signed off and before the exposure was computed.
  • Every figure has a trail. Each number on this page is tied to ledger lines, a spine file row, a questionnaire line or a published price file, and each ledger line carries its data-row and factor citation in Northwood's format.
  • Stale flags. Company figures are flagged because FY2025 is the latest collected year and the report date (2026-10-02) is more than 90 days after it; the next annual collection is the update. Emission factors carry their vintage (EPA Hub 2025, grid-average electricity factors from EPA eGRID 2023) and are not flagged.

Stated limitations

  • Market-based Scope 2 is not calculated because no company reported contract data. It could be higher or lower than the location-based figure: renewable contracts would lower it, while without contracts it uses the residual grid mix, which is often higher than the grid average.
  • Scope 3 is not collected. Total value-chain emissions are larger than Scope 1 and 2 by an amount this inventory cannot size (section 6).
  • Emissions data has not been independently verified by a third party. A buyer may ask for third-party assurance; the grades in section 3 show where it would start. Scope and cost are not estimated here and would be Northwood's decision.
