01The answer

Ten companies, one question: what moves what a buyer pays?

We connected each company's footprint to its financials, sized the exposures in dollars, ranked them, and costed the response. This is what it says.

The ten companies carry $13.5M a year of expected loss: $7.9M on earnings they already have and $5.6M on revenue not yet won. 12Caveats

Nine things to keep in mind when you use these numbers

The work is complete as it stands; these are the limits of what it can tell you.

Exit dates are Kith's modelEach is the report date plus the remaining hold, with 4 held at a one-year floor. Which exposures fall inside a hold depends on it. Where Northwood's own plan differs, use that.
Owners and first moves are proposalsThey come from roles named in the company files, adjacent roles, or Kith's own suggestion, each tagged by basis. None was confirmed with a company.
Cost to respond is an estimate †Built from two benchmark pages. The paybacks in days assume the half-lost case, which is an illustration, not a forecast.
Revenue not yet won is never bankedHope-to-win revenue sizes what is at stake. It is not in earnings and is never counted toward exit value.
Estimates fill the gaps, and say so51 of 56 exposure lines rest on Kith estimates (grade C); 13 of them were built from published benchmarks where the data room was silent. All are marked † and tagged. They are low confidence: they rank and size, they do not quote.
How the totals workThe company total is the sum of its lines, leaving out the carbon-price scenario because it overlaps the energy line. Lines can strike in the same year, so the total is an average expected loss, not a worst case.
A Harvest Ridge drought line on simulated valuesThe drought curtailment line rests in part on simulated plant values, by a specific exception to the rule that keeps simulated data out of other figures; it is a scenario, not an observed drought link, and the water authority attributed the 2025 pressure drops to demand and maintenance. The water price line uses the reported 28M gallons.
Patriot's exit is the base caseIts files give 2027 to 2028 as a target and the first half of 2029 as the base case; the model uses 2029 because the target needs $32M to $38M of EBITDA against $23.8M today. A 2027 exit would shorten its hold.
Totals are cases, not forecasts$13.5M is expected loss with all exposed customer revenue lost and $9.0M at half lost; $5.6M of the total is revenue not yet won.

14Back-end note: how it was built

Everything in the build is one of six things, and each number has a trail

This note is for whoever audits or maintains the work. It shows what each part of the process does and how the parts link, without opening a single script. Counts are the record as at 11 October 2026, before the final (G7) freeze.

Rules

The charter's 16 rules say what must be true: units, scenarios, bands, missing data, ranking, simulated data and the customer data requirement. Nothing on a page departs from them unless a dated decision says so.

Scripts

24 run-order steps (87 script versions are kept) build every file from the data room and the reference data. They stop with a plain message if an input, header or period no longer matches, and never overwrite.

Checks

Gate checks re-derive figures from source and fail on a defect; each is proved with seeded failures before it is trusted (33 test files). A check that passes is stronger evidence than a reviewer's opinion.

Reviewers

Reviewer agents, each read-only and given one question, work from the same packet: Evidence review, Audience read, Presentation review, Method review, Process review. They raise findings; they cannot change a file.

Decisions

180 decisions are logged with the date and channel, and the owner's words wherever the owner decided. A builder's proposal is marked proposed until the owner ratifies it.

The record

289 entries are linked to one another, so an earlier entry cannot be altered without breaking the chain. 259 journal entries say what was done, why, and what was left open.

G0 · Process review must-fix

Caught: A five-year forward hold was applied to every company, but Sterling is already 5.7 years in and Meridian exits in the first quarter of 2027.

Done: Hold years are now set per company from its documented exit plan.

G1 · Evidence review must-fix

Caught: Clearview's weather events were counted over one year when the company lists three, which overstated how likely they are.

Done: The window is now the three years the company reports.

G4 · Evidence review must-fix

Caught: Ridgeline's growth-plan investment was stated in its own plan, but the model said it was not stated.

Done: Quantified from the plan's own investment table and shown as a company plan, not capitalised.

G4 · Method review must-fix

Caught: Ridgeline's weather impact had been taken as a property owner's cost, not Ridgeline's own.

Done: Carried as Ridgeline's own small event; the owner-borne losses are shown as context.

G4 · Method review must-fix

Caught: Pinnacle's largest event had combined two storms, lifting its severity band.

Done: Apportioned by closure days; the combined figure is shown only as an upper bound.

G5 · Audience read must-fix

Caught: Exit dates were labelled 'planned exit' though they are Kith's model, not a Northwood plan.

Done: Relabelled 'modelled exit', with floors marked as the earliest modelled date.

G5 · Audience read must-fix

Caught: Paybacks in days and exit values read as sized benefits though they rest on a what-if.

Done: Labelled 'if the half-lost case happens' and 'what-if, not likelihood-weighted'.
